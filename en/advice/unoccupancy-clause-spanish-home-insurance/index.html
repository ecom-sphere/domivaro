What your Spanish insurance requires when the house stands empty

We opened 17 home insurance policies sold in Spain to answer one simple question: does your cover change when the house is empty? The answer is not the one printed everywhere.

Published 7 August 2026 · 6 minute read

What is false, and printed everywhere

In the United States this trade is sold with one line: «your insurer requires an inspection every 7 to 14 days, or your cover is void». We wanted to know whether that held in Spain. We opened 17 policies.

The answer is no. None of the 17 Spanish policies we read requires periodic inspections. We searched the full text of each for inspección, visita periódica, revisión periódica and custodia de las llaves. The only hits are unrelated.

We say it because the American line circulates in Spanish and in English on websites in our own trade. If someone tells you that, ring your insurer: they will tell you what we just did.

What Spanish contracts actually say is different, and for an absent owner it is more demanding. They do not require a visit. They require an act.

In short

None of the 17 Spanish policies we read requires periodic inspections: the American argument is false here. But 7 contracts require an act, from 48 hours at Caser to 60 days at Ibex, usually turning the mains water off and draining. Articles 11 and 12 of law 50/1980 cut the payout pro rata if the increased risk was never declared.

What 7 contracts require, and from when

Every line is a clause we read in the general conditions published by the insurer itself. The thresholds run from 48 hours to 60 days.

The last line matters most to you. The Ibex and HDI Spain Holiday Home policy, held by many foreign owners, defines the Unoccupied state as 60 consecutive days with no presence «of any person you have entrusted with a key to Your Home». In other words, a keyholder's visit resets the clock. It is the only one of the 17 where our trade appears inside the contractual definition.

What the law adds, and few people know

Beyond the contract, the Spanish insurance contract act imposes a duty and sets a penalty.

article 11

you must tell your insurer about any change that increases the risk, as soon as you reasonably can

pro rata

if you did not and a claim arises, the payout is cut in the ratio between the premium paid and the one that should have applied

100 %

the insurer is released entirely if bad faith is established

Article 12 is blunt: « En el caso de que el tomador del seguro o el asegurado no haya efectuado su declaración y sobreviniere un siniestro, el asegurador queda liberado de su prestación si el tomador o el asegurado ha actuado con mala fe. En otro caso, la prestación del asegurador se reducirá proporcionalmente a la diferencia entre la prima convenida y la que se hubiera aplicado de haberse conocido la verdadera entidad del riesgo ».

In plain terms: if your policy was written for a main residence and the house is actually empty 9 months a year, the gap will not show while nothing happens. It will show on the day of the claim.

4 acts that stand up to a loss adjuster

These are the ones the contracts name. They need no special skill, only being there and being able to prove it.

1

Turn off the mains water

The most cost effective single measure there is. It turns a burst hose into a non-event, and 4 of the 7 contracts in the table name it expressly.

2

Drain whatever can freeze

Water heater, outside pipes, tanks. Zurich, Allianz and Caser require it explicitly beyond their threshold, and the omission alone is enough to exclude the claim.

3

Photograph the date

A closed tap with no proof is worth nothing to an adjuster. What counts is a time stamped photograph of the closed tap, attached to a dated report.

4

Declare how the home is really used

A policy taken out for a main residence on a house that is empty 9 months a year is an undeclared increase in risk. An email to your insurer is enough, and it costs less than a pro rata cut.

4 questions about your cover

How do I find out what my own policy says?

Ask your insurer or broker in writing for the exact wording of the desocupación or deshabitación clause, and from how many days it bites. Ask for the answer by email, not on the phone. It is a routine request and they answer within days.

My house is empty 9 months a year. Am I less covered?

It depends entirely on your policy and on what you declared when you took it out. With some insurers simple theft drops away after 10 days of absence, with others nothing changes. The only way to know is to read your general conditions, or have them read.

If I turn the water off, do I still need visits?

Turning the water off removes internal water damage, which is the most frequent and most expensive claim. It does nothing about a roof leak, a blocked terrace drain, a leak on the outside drip line or a break-in. And water left off all year dries out the traps, which brings drain smells back up into the house.

Is a photo report enough for a loss adjuster?

We cannot promise you that; it depends on the claim and the company. What we do know is that a file with certain dates and time stamped photographs is argued differently from a bare statement, and that several policies expressly hang cover on an act that only dated proof can establish.

What to remember

  • No Spanish policy requires periodic inspections. If anyone tells you otherwise, call your insurer.
  • 7 of 17 contracts require a dated act, from 48 hours to 60 days of absence, usually turning the water off and draining.
  • The Ibex and HDI expatriate policy makes a keyholder's visit a contractual fact that stops the house turning Unoccupied.
  • An undeclared increase in risk cuts the payout pro rata, and wipes it out entirely where bad faith is shown.
